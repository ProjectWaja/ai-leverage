This is the floor. It's where almost everyone starts, and where most people stay without realizing there's anywhere else to go.

## Where most people are

You type a request — "write a function that validates emails," "fix this bug," "build me a landing page" — copy whatever comes out, paste it into your editor, and move on. If it doesn't work, you either patch it by hand or shrug and decide the AI "isn't there yet."

The mental model is a vending machine: insert request, receive product. The interaction is over the moment you get an answer.

## Why it caps your results

A vending machine can't ask what you actually want. So you get the *average* answer to an *underspecified* question:

- No context about your codebase, so the code uses conventions you don't follow.
- No back-and-forth, so the first guess is the final answer — and first guesses to vague questions are mediocre by definition.
- No verification, so you find out it's wrong at the worst possible time.

The model isn't underpowered. It's under-informed and under-directed, and you ended the conversation before either could be fixed.

## The tell

You catch yourself thinking any of these:

- "AI is fine for boilerplate but useless for real work."
- "I spend more time fixing its code than writing my own."
- "It doesn't understand my project."

All three are real experiences. All three are symptoms of Level 0, not verdicts on the tool.

## The unlock → [Level 1: The Conversation](1-conversation.md)

Stop treating the first answer as the answer. The single highest-leverage habit in this entire guide is also the easiest: **when the output is wrong, say what's wrong and go again.** That one move turns a vending machine into a collaborator.
